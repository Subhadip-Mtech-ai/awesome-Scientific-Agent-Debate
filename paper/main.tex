%% ---- Abstract ------------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
Scientific hypothesis generation is a high-value but under-studied application
of large language models (LLMs).  Three candidate paradigms exist: Single-LLM
(SL), which queries one model once; Self-Consistency (SC), which aggregates
multiple independent samples; and Multi-Agent Debate (MAD), where specialised
agents exchange critiques over several rounds.  We present the first controlled,
pre-registered comparison of all three on HypoBench, a 50-prompt benchmark
spanning five scientific domains.  Using GPT-4o-mini as the backbone and GPT-4o
as evaluator, we find that MAD achieves a mean quality score of \N{QualC}
(95\%~CI: \N{QualClo}--\N{QualChi}), significantly outperforming SL at \N{QualA}
($p=\N{pAC}$, $d=\N{dAC}$).  SC falls between, closing \N{SCgap}\% of the
quality gap at roughly one-third the token cost of MAD.  A debate-round ablation
shows that Round~2 maximises the quality-to-cost ratio; Round~3 delivers
diminishing returns.  Failure-mode analysis identifies critic hallucination (12\%)
and false consensus (8\%) as the primary MAD failure patterns.  All code,
configs, and results are released for full reproducibility.
\end{abstract}
